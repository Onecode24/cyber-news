Beginner Pick — Learn the Fundamentals

The Colonial Pipeline attack: how one leaked password shut down fuel for half the US East Coast

An evergreen story worth knowing, not breaking news — from May 2021.

In May 2021, a ransomware gang called DarkSide broke into Colonial Pipeline, which supplies nearly half the fuel used on the US East Coast, using a single compromised VPN password that had no multi-factor authentication protecting it. The hackers only got into the company's billing and business network, not the pipeline's physical control systems, but Colonial shut the pipeline down anyway out of caution — and that shutdown, not the hack itself, is what caused days of gas station lines, panic buying, and regional fuel shortages.

Colonial paid roughly $4.4 million in Bitcoin to get a decryption tool, and the US Justice Department later recovered a large chunk of that ransom by tracing the cryptocurrency wallet — an early sign that ransomware payments aren't as untraceable as attackers assume. The incident is a favorite teaching example because it shows two things a newcomer to the field should internalize early: attackers rarely need to touch the "important" systems directly to cause massive real-world damage, and basic hygiene like MFA on remote access is often the difference between a contained incident and a national news story.
