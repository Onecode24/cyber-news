Beginner Pick — Educational Feature

The MGM Resorts hack that started with a phone call

Not breaking news — a formative case worth understanding — September 2023

In September 2023, a hacking group called Scattered Spider brought one of the world's largest casino operators to its knees — and they didn't write a single line of exploit code to get in. They found an MGM employee's profile on LinkedIn, called MGM's IT help desk pretending to be that employee, and in a ten-minute conversation talked the help desk into resetting the employee's multi-factor authentication. That was the whole break-in.

From there, the attackers used that reset to gain administrator access to MGM's identity system (Okta) and its cloud infrastructure. The fallout was dramatic and very physical: slot machines went dark, hotel key cards stopped working, guests were locked out of their rooms, and MGM's booking systems and website went offline for over a week. The company later estimated the incident cost it around $100 million.

The reason this story is a great starting point for learning security: it wasn't a sophisticated technical exploit that got MGM, it was a process failure. A single unverified phone call carried enough trust to unlock accounts protected by MFA — the very control designed to stop exactly this kind of takeover.

Why it matters for you: Security isn't only firewalls and passwords — it's also the humans and processes with the power to override protections. Whenever you're asked to "just verify your identity" over the phone or reset a security setting for someone, slow down and confirm who you're really talking to.
