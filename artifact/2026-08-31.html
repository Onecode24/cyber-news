Beginner Pick · Learn the Backstory

The MGM Resorts hack: how a 10-minute phone call took down a casino empire

In September 2023, a group of young, mostly English-speaking hackers known as Scattered Spider didn't break any code to get into MGM Resorts. They called the company's IT help desk, pretended to be an employee locked out of their account, and talked their way into a password reset. That single conversation gave them a foothold that let them escalate access across MGM's network, deploy ransomware, and shut down slot machines, hotel key cards, and reservation systems at properties across the US for over a week.

The outage reportedly cost MGM over $100 million. Rival casino operator Caesars Entertainment was hit by the same group days earlier and chose to pay a ransom to avoid a similar shutdown.

This story is a great first lesson in security because it shows that the weakest link usually isn't a flaw in the code — it's a person following a normal-sounding request. This kind of trick is called social engineering, and it's why many companies now require extra proof of identity (like a manager callback or video verification) before resetting anyone's credentials, no matter how convincing the caller sounds.

Why it matters for beginners: it's one of the clearest real examples of how "hacking" is often about manipulating people and processes, not just computers.
