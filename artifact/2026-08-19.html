MGM Resorts, 2023: the $100 million breach that started with a phone call

In September 2023, a hacking group called Scattered Spider brought one of the world's largest casino operators to its knees — not with clever malware, but with a single phone call. They found an MGM employee's name on LinkedIn, then called the company's IT help desk pretending to be that employee, and talked their way into a password reset. That was enough to get a foothold inside MGM's network.

From there, the attackers escalated their access and eventually deployed ransomware across MGM's systems. Slot machines went dark, hotel room key cards stopped working, and reservation and payment systems failed for roughly ten days. MGM later estimated the incident cost the company around $100 million in lost business and cleanup, even though it refused to pay the ransom.

Why it matters for beginners: it's a textbook example of social engineering — manipulating a person rather than breaking code. No firewall or antivirus stops a convincing phone call to a help desk. It's also why many organizations now require callback verification and stronger identity checks before any password reset, and why "the weakest link is usually human" has become one of security's most repeated (and truest) lessons.
