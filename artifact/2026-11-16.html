The Colonial Pipeline Attack

May 2021 · Ransomware

In May 2021, a ransomware gang called DarkSide broke into the IT network of Colonial Pipeline, the operator of the largest fuel pipeline in the United States, using a single compromised password for an old VPN account that had no multi-factor authentication. The attackers never touched the pipeline's physical control systems — they hit the billing and business network. But Colonial shut the pipeline down anyway, because it couldn't be sure the attack hadn't spread, and because it had no way to bill customers for fuel it delivered.

The shutdown cut off nearly half the fuel supply to the US East Coast for several days, triggering panic buying, mile-long gas station lines, and a brief spike in prices. Colonial ended up paying the attackers roughly $4.4 million in Bitcoin to get a decryption key, and the US Department of Justice later clawed back a portion of that ransom by tracing the cryptocurrency wallet it moved through.

Why it matters for beginners: this incident is the textbook example of how a ransomware attack doesn't need to touch the "important" systems to cause real-world chaos — a weak password and no MFA on a forgotten account was enough. It's also one of the clearest public demonstrations that paying a ransom in cryptocurrency doesn't guarantee anonymity, and that basic hygiene (MFA, network segmentation, retiring old accounts) is often the actual line of defense.
