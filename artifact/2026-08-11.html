    A 2021 firmware integration error in Coldcard hardware wallets used the wrong random-number generator when creating wallet seeds, letting attackers predict them. For newcomers · why this matters

The MGM Resorts breach: hacking without touching a keyboard

In September 2023, a group known as Scattered Spider took down MGM Resorts' casino floors, hotel systems, and slot machines for over a week — costing the company more than $100 million. They didn't use a zero-day exploit or crack any passwords. They called MGM's IT help desk, impersonated an employee found on LinkedIn, and talked their way into a password reset.

That single phone call gave them a foothold to escalate privileges and spread through MGM's network, eventually deploying ransomware. Caesars Entertainment, hit by the same group days earlier, quietly paid a reported $15 million ransom rather than risk the same outage.

Why it matters for beginners: it's a clean example of "social engineering" — attacking the humans and processes around a system instead of the system itself. No firewall or antivirus stops a helpful help-desk agent who's been fooled. It's a good reminder that identity verification, employee training, and process design are as much a part of security as code.
