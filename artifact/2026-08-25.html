Federal patch deadline lands today for an already-exploited Windows kernel bug

CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free flaw in the Windows WinSock driver (afd.sys) that lets a local attacker jump to SYSTEM, was already being exploited when Microsoft patched it on August 11. Under CISA's binding directive, today — August 25 — is the deadline for US federal civilian agencies to have it fixed; everyone else running Windows should treat it the same way.

Beginner Pick

Not breaking news — a foundational story worth knowing

The Change Healthcare Ransomware Attack

February 2024

In February 2024, attackers linked to the ALPHV/BlackCat ransomware gang broke into Change Healthcare — a company that quietly processes claims and payments for a huge share of the US healthcare system — through a remote-access portal that had no multi-factor authentication. They spent nine days moving through the network undetected before locking systems and stealing an estimated 6 terabytes of data, including medical records and Social Security numbers for over 100 million people.

The fallout was enormous: pharmacies couldn't process prescriptions, hospitals couldn't submit insurance claims, and many providers went weeks without being paid. UnitedHealth Group (Change Healthcare's parent company) paid the attackers roughly $22 million — and the criminals leaked the stolen data anyway. The total cost to UnitedHealth has run into the billions.

It's a foundational story for beginners for two reasons: it shows how one missing basic control — multi-factor authentication on a single remote-access point — can cascade into a crisis for an entire industry, and it's a sobering real-world example of why paying a ransom is never a guarantee that stolen data stays private.

Why we picked it now: Medusa's advisory above lists healthcare among its top targets, and both incidents trace back to the same root cause — an internet-facing system without MFA. If you remember one lesson from this issue, make it that one.
