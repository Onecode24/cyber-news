Beginner Pick — Learn the Fundamentals

The MGM Resorts hack: how a 10-minute phone call took down a casino empire

Not breaking news — a landmark incident from September 2023, still one of the clearest lessons in security today

In September 2023, a hacker from the group known as Scattered Spider called MGM Resorts' IT helpdesk, pretended to be a locked-out employee, and talked their way into a password reset — no malware, no exploit code, just a convincing phone call. That single reset gave the attackers access to MGM's Okta identity system, which controlled logins across the company's casinos and hotels.

The fallout was dramatic: slot machines went dark, digital room keys stopped working, and online booking systems froze for about ten days, costing MGM more than $100 million. MGM chose not to pay the ransom, following guidance from law enforcement and security experts.

Why it matters for beginners: this attack didn't break any encryption or exploit any software bug — it exploited a person, and a process, on a helpdesk that was too willing to trust a phone call. It's a textbook example of "social engineering," and it's exactly the same playbook (vishing, or voice phishing) attackers used against Abbott Laboratories in this week's headlines above. The lesson scales down to anyone: verify identity before resetting credentials, and treat unexpected "urgent" requests — even ones that sound legitimate — with healthy suspicion.
