Colonial Pipeline: how one leaked password shut down fuel to the East Coast

In May 2021, the ransomware group DarkSide broke into Colonial Pipeline, which carries nearly half the fuel supply for the U.S. East Coast, through a single compromised VPN password for an account that had no multi-factor authentication. The attackers encrypted the company's billing and business systems. Colonial itself took the pipeline offline as a precaution, even though the operational control systems that actually pump fuel were never touched.

The shutdown lasted about six days and triggered real gas shortages and panic buying across the Southeast. Colonial paid roughly $4.4 million in Bitcoin to get a decryption key; the U.S. Department of Justice later recovered about $2.3 million of that ransom by tracing the cryptocurrency wallet.

Why it matters for beginners: the breach didn't require advanced hacking, just one reused or stolen password on an account without MFA. It's a textbook case of how a small, boring gap in "basic" security hygiene can cascade into a national crisis, and why MFA and credential hygiene get so much emphasis in cybersecurity fundamentals.
