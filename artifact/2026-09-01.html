For newcomers

Beginner Pick: The Colonial Pipeline attack

Educational feature — not breaking news

In May 2021, a ransomware gang called DarkSide broke into Colonial Pipeline, the company operating the largest fuel pipeline on the US East Coast, using a single compromised password for an old VPN account that didn't even have multi-factor authentication turned on. The company shut the pipeline down itself as a precaution, and the six-day outage triggered fuel shortages and panic-buying across the southeastern United States.

What makes this case such a good first lesson is the gap between cause and effect: the entry point was almost embarrassingly ordinary — one leaked credential, no MFA — but the consequence was a national news story and a $4.4 million ransom payment (part of which the FBI later recovered). It's a clean illustration of why basic hygiene like MFA and password rotation matters so much, why "IT security" and "critical infrastructure safety" are the same problem, and why ransomware gangs increasingly go after operators of physical infrastructure, not just data.

It also pushed ransomware from a back-office IT problem to a matter of presidential briefings — the US government issued new pipeline cybersecurity regulations within weeks. If you remember one story about why cybersecurity fundamentals matter beyond the office, this is a good one to know.
