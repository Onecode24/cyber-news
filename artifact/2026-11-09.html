Beginner Pick

Learn from history — not breaking news

The Colonial Pipeline attack: how one password shut down fuel for the East Coast

May 2021 · Ransomware · Critical infrastructure

In May 2021, a ransomware gang called DarkSide broke into Colonial Pipeline, which carries almost half the fuel used on the U.S. East Coast, using a single leaked password on an old VPN account that didn't have multi-factor authentication turned on. The password turned up in a batch of credentials leaked from an unrelated breach — the attackers didn't even need to guess it.

Colonial didn't wait to find out how far the ransomware had spread into its control systems. It shut the entire pipeline down as a precaution, which triggered gas shortages, panic buying, and price spikes across several states — a striking example of a purely digital attack causing very physical, very visible consequences. The company ended up paying the attackers about $4.4 million in Bitcoin to get a decryption key.

The story has a rare twist: the U.S. Department of Justice later traced and recovered roughly $2.3 million of that ransom by following the Bitcoin through the blockchain to a wallet the attackers controlled. For newcomers, this case is a tidy lesson in three ideas that show up constantly in security: how one reused or leaked credential can be enough to get in, why multi-factor authentication matters, and why law enforcement increasingly treats cryptocurrency tracing as a real tool rather than a dead end.
