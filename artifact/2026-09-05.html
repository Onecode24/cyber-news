Beginner Pick · a story worth understanding, not breaking news

The XZ Utils backdoor: a supply-chain attack caught almost by accident

In early 2024, a Microsoft engineer named Andres Freund noticed something odd while benchmarking his computer: SSH logins, a routine way to connect securely to remote servers, were taking a fraction of a second longer than they should. Most people would have shrugged it off — he investigated instead.

What he found was a deliberately planted backdoor hidden inside XZ Utils, a small, widely used open-source compression tool that quietly ships inside countless Linux systems. Over roughly two years, an anonymous contributor had built trust with the project's maintainer under a fake identity, then slipped in malicious code designed to let an attacker with the right secret key bypass SSH authentication entirely on affected servers.

Had it gone undetected, the backdoor could have given attackers a hidden master key into an enormous number of servers worldwide — one of the most severe supply-chain compromises ever attempted. It was caught only because one engineer trusted a half-second delay enough to dig in.

The lesson for newcomers: modern software is built on layers of open-source components that few people scrutinize line by line, and trust in those components can be quietly exploited over long periods. It's also a reminder that unglamorous habits — noticing when something is slightly slower or slightly "off" — are a real and valuable security skill.
