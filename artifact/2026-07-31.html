For newcomers

The Colonial Pipeline attack: how one stolen password shut down fuel for the East Coast

In May 2021, a ransomware gang called DarkSide broke into Colonial Pipeline, the company that carries nearly half the fuel used on the U.S. East Coast — using a single leaked password to an old VPN account that didn't even have two-factor authentication turned on.

Colonial shut the pipeline down itself as a precaution while it dealt with the intrusion, but the six-day outage was enough to trigger gas shortages and panic-buying up and down the coast. The company ended up paying about $4.4 million in ransom; the FBI later recovered a large chunk of that cryptocurrency, an unusually good outcome for a ransomware case.

It's one of the clearest examples of how a purely financial cybercrime — attackers just wanted money, not to sabotage fuel supplies — aimed only at office IT systems can still cascade into real-world, physical disruption. It's also a big reason "turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere, including old accounts you forgot about" became universal security advice.
