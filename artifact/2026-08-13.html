Beginner Pick — Learn the Fundamentals

Operation Cronos: the day police took over a ransomware gang's own website

Not breaking news — a landmark takedown from February 2024, worth knowing as you learn how the defense side fights back.

By early 2024, LockBit was the world's most prolific ransomware gang — a "ransomware-as-a-service" operation that had hit more than 2,000 victims and collected over $120 million in ransoms by renting its malware and leak-site infrastructure out to criminal affiliates. On February 19–20, 2024, law enforcement from ten countries — led by the UK's National Crime Agency and the FBI — pulled off Operation Cronos: they seized 34 servers across eight countries, took control of LockBit's dark-web leak site and admin panel, recovered decryption keys for victims, froze over 200 cryptocurrency accounts, and arrested affiliates in Poland and Ukraine.

What makes this story especially fun for newcomers is what police did next: instead of just shutting the site down, they repurposed LockBit's own leak page to publish their own countdown timers, press releases, and mocking messages using the gang's branding against it. It's a great first case study in two ideas that matter a lot in security: cryptocurrency payments are traceable with enough resources, and disrupting an attacker's infrastructure (not just patching your own systems) is a real, powerful part of cyber defense — even against a criminal enterprise built to survive.
