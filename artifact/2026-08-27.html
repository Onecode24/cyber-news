Colonial Pipeline (2021): one leaked password shut down fuel for half the US East Coast

In May 2021, a ransomware gang called DarkSide broke into Colonial Pipeline, the company operating the largest fuel pipeline in the United States. They got in through a single VPN account whose password had leaked online and wasn't protected by multi-factor authentication (MFA) — no phishing email, no zero-day exploit, just a reused password on an account nobody thought to double-lock.

Colonial shut the pipeline down itself to contain the attack, which triggered gas shortages and panic-buying across several states. The company ultimately paid the attackers roughly $4.4 million in Bitcoin, part of which the FBI later recovered.

Why it matters for beginners: it's the clearest real-world proof that "boring" basics — unique passwords and MFA on every remote-access account — aren't optional advice, they're the difference between a non-event and a national headline.
